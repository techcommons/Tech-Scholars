"You teach the heart as well as the mind. Sometimes the truth is hard to bear, but it is better to walk in the light of truth than to live in the darkness of lies."

Zitkála-Šá became one of the most influential Native American female activists of her time. She spoke out against unfair government policies and fought for Native Americans to have the same rights as other U.S. citizens. She helped build support for the Indian Citizenship Act of 1924, which granted U.S. citizenship to all Native Americans born in the United States. The same year, she co-authored a report called "Oklahoma's Poor Rich Indians" that exposed the exploitation and corruption surrounding the Osage population's oil wealth.

In 1926, she founded the National Council of American Indians, an organization that advocated for voting rights, better education and healthcare, and the preservation of tribal sovereignty. She continued this work until her death, becoming one of the leading voices for Indigenous rights in the United States. Zitkála-Šá remains an inspiration for many to this day.
