Activism Through Literature in the Fight for Racial Equality

Phillis Wheatley: American Revolution Era, and Abolition

Phillis Wheatley, at the young age of seven was taking through The Middle Passage, and was sold to a white family. The family lived in poverty, and abused Phillis's writing skills, and sold her work, slowly growing in wealth. Despite her having fame for being a talented writer, she was still enslaved. Eventually, the Wheatleys send Phillis to London to publish, as she could not do it in the colonies. When she arrived in London, due to the differences in the laws there, she was no longer enslaved– becoming a free person as there was a law that enslavers could not force the people they enslaved back to the colonies.

She eventually published more poetry, discussing her opposition to slavery. In a letter (formulated like a poem) she sent to Mary Wooster, after hearing of her husband's death in battle, she wrote "While yet (O deed ungenerous!) they disgrace/And hold in bondage Africa's blameless race…"

IMG: Phillis Wheatley to Mary Wooster, letter, July 15, 1778. Collection of the Massachusetts Historical Society.

Angelina Weld Grimké: Poetry and Plays Against Supremacism

Grimké was an African American poet, writer, and playwright, using her writing to advocate for racial equality. Her most famous play, 'Rachel' was used to combat the white supremacist film 'The Birth of a Nation' and its propaganda. The play discussed lynching, with the main character– Rachel, losing her father and half brother to the crime . The play was written to bring light to the struggles of African Americans in a white society, and to bring more African American theatre to the world, especially through political activism with the NAACP. She wrote other plays, although never published, 'The Closing Door' also dealt with lynching and the intentional killing of infants, also called infanticide.

IMG: Weld Grimké, Angelina, 'Rachel', 1920

Authors Statement

After looking at the success of African American women writing in order to fight for Racial Equality, we see how literature being used as an activism source has existed for years upon years, and continues into today. The usage of writing not only empowers women, but it puts their voices into paper, something that can be preserved forever.
