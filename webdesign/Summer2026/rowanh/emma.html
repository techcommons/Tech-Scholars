Sarah Emma Edmonds

Image credit: American Battlefield Trust, "Sarah Emma Edmonds" .

Sarah Emma Edmonds was born to an abusive father, who would often beat and attack her because of her gender. He had been hoping for a son, so when Sarah was born female, he took his anger out on her, often beating and yelling and blaming her for the family's misfortunes. To escape this abuse, Sarah took the identity of a man, and ran away to a small town in her native country Canada. She then moved to the states in an attempt to get further away from her Family, and wound up joining the army under the name Franklin Thomas, and fought in many famous battles throughout the revolutionary war. While serving as a soldier, she participated in several major battles and earned a reputation for being brave and dependable. Some accounts also claim that she worked as a spy by disguising herself in different ways to gather information from Confederate forces, though historians debate which of these stories are true. Eventually, she became ill and left the army because seeking medical treatment would have exposed her identity. After the war, she wrote a memoir about her experiences, and years later she became one of the few women to receive a military pension for her service, leaving behind a legacy of courage and determination.
