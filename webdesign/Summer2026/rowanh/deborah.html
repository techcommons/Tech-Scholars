    • Deborah Sampson (1760–1827) was an extremely brave and patriotic woman during the Revolutionary War. She was so patriotic, in fact, that she took advantage of her masculine features, disguised herself as a man, and enlisted in the Continental Army under the name Robert Shurtliff. During her time in the army, she was assigned to many dangerous tasks under the leadership of Captain George Webb, which she completed with great efficiency and accuracy, all while keeping the truth of her gender a complete secret. She kept up the act for two whole years, during which she took a bullet to the leg, a sword slash to the head, and many other brutal injuries. In the end, she was discovered as a woman while being treated for an illness. After receiving an honorable discharge from the army, Deborah returned to civilian life, where she married Benjamin Gannett and raised a family. She later traveled throughout New England giving public lectures about her experiences in the war, becoming one of the first American women to make a career as a public speaker. With the support of leaders such as Paul Revere, she was eventually granted a military pension in recognition of her service, and today she is remembered as one of the most courageous women to fight for American independence.
