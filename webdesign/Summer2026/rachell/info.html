"The unusual, especially in sexual anomalies, is what the public wants to read with breakfast coffee."

Christine Jorgensen, born May 30th, 1952, grew up in the Bronx, New York. She was a singer, actress, transgender activist, and the first person to gain fame from having sex reassignment surgery. After a news article released showing her transition, she became a national celebrity. With this newfound publicity came hatred but also a new sense of public awareness for trans identities in America. In fact, she was offered tons of new opportunities to go on talk shows, perform in clubs, and talk in lectures. Although Jorgensen was initially overwhelmed by her new fame, she decided to use this notoriety as a way to spread awareness and understanding for transgender people. She spoke about her experiences to medically transition but also highlighted the importance of an inner one. She especially highlighted the freedom and joy which transitioning brought her.

"You don't do it primarily for sexual reasons, you do it because of who you are."

However, this is not to say that her relatively good reception from the American public meant that transgender people became accepted at this time. Jorgensen's fame was likely supported by the fact that she perfectly fit the standards of womanhood for America at this time. She was white, had blonde hair and blue eyes, demonstrated advanced medical technology of the modern world, and represented American values through her prior service in the army and portrayal by the media in domestic roles. This made her palatable enough to be a spectacle to the public rather than someone that could be stripped down to someone mentally ill. She acknowledged the expectations placed on her herself stating that "unlike other women I had to become super-female. I couldn't have a single masculine trait." At times, she even contradicted herself such as sometimes justifying her gender to the public through her medical surgeries as opposed to the internal transition she would emphasize at a different time.

Of course, her positive effect cannot be understated—her publicity not only brought transgender people to the public consciousness, but she also acted as a role model for many, receiving tons of letters from other transgender people asking her for advice. Moreover, she published an extremely successful autobiography with around 450,000 copies, giving a voice to the trans woman experience.

This is all to say that Christine Jorgensen's life is extremely important to discuss when talking about transgender history in America as it exposed the public at the time to trans people but also shows us today the strength it took for a trans person to live in a society which failed to recognize them at all. It is also important to acknowledge however that Jorgensen's life embodied a very small percentage of the lives of transgender Americans at this time. Therefore, her life should be taken as a reminder to learn stories like Jorgensen's but also note the existence of other American transgender women at this time who were erased from trans narratives. With a strong understanding of transgender history, we can all move forward with greater understanding and empathy for ourselves and those around us.
