"It has been said many times that one can learn a great deal about a society by looking towards its prisons. Look towards its dungeons and there you will see in concentrated and microcosmic form the sickness of the entire system." — Angela Davis, 1972


In 1970, professor and activist Angela Davis became one of the most visible political prisoners in American history after being charged with murder, kidnapping, and conspiracy in connection with a shooting that she was never even present for.


Her subsequent arrest, imprisonment, and eventual acquittal sparked an international movement rallying around the cry "Free Angela Davis."


Her case, and the movement it inspired, created a public reckoning over not merely the criminal justice system as a whole, but the specific challenges Black women experience within it.
