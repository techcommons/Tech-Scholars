Mabel Lee

It is important to recognize, however, that Mabel Lee was particularly educated and privileged for her time. Her father was a Baptist missionary who worked in the United States for the duration of Lee's childhood. Lee herself received excellent education at a missionary school and received the Boxer Indemnity Scholarship which allowed her to study abroad in the United States. This scholarship is interesting as its goal was to foster US-China relations by educating Chinese intellectuals to then return home and modernize China. It shows how despite Lee's privilege to be educated, the intersection of her race and gender in a society with exclusionary sentiments led to much oppression. Despite this, eventually Lee became the first woman to earn a PhD in Economics, which she earned at Columbia University.

Interestingly, Lee lived in New York City and was involved in a lot of activism. She is well known for appearing on horseback in a 1912 NYC suffrage march at just sixteen years. She was a symbol of women of color and the possibilities of women's suffrage in a parade of nearly 10,000 people organized by the Women's Political Union. Rumors spread that China had legalized women's suffrage and hence Lee was used as a symbol to almost shame Americans into believing they should and are better and hence need women's suffrage. This larger intent underscores disparities when it comes to race within the women's suffrage movement. The New York Tribune reads, "Regarding her as the symbol of the new era, when all their women will be free and unhampered". It can be interpreted that she was exoticized and almost tokenized as rhetoric rather than actually being framed an equal advocate.

It's important to note that even after women earned the right to vote in 1920, many Chinese American women could not vote because they could not become citizens. It wasn't until 1943 when the Chinese Exclusion Act was repealed that most Chinese American women had an equal opportunity to vote. Still, Lee encouraged Asian American women to get an education and stay civically involved, showing a powerful example of selfless political participation.

Lee went on to study at Barnard College as a History and Philosophy major, Columbia Teachers College, and earned her PhD at Columbia in Economics. Her dissertation challenged western views of modernization that viewed Chinese society as behind and stagnant and instead she showed how not only were Chinese people active participants in society and the economy, so were women like her.
