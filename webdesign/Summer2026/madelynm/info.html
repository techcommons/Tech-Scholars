Arrival of the Brides

Click on the painting to explore more on the WAMS website!

Eleanor Fortescue-Brickdale's Arrival of the Brides depicts women dressed in opulent clothing and jewelry as they present themselves to the men of their new society. The accessories of these brides suggests their high socioeconomic status to the gentlemen in the painting. This detail sets these women in particular up to be married to the upper-class men of New France, while women who dress less decadently would be married to their appropriate socioeconomic match in the colony. Brickdale made this piece in the 1800s, but the painting does not accurately deoict the men's attitude toward the women. Men often would speak of women as cattle, equating them to property. This set the stage for women's role in society as being second to men. In a more modern lens, filles du roi were the keystone to New France's success as they were brought to sustain and grow the population.

Jean Talon's Letter

On October 27, 1667, Jean Talon, the intendant of New France, wrote a letter to Jean-Baptiste Colbert, the French statesman. In this letter, Talon confirms that the initial cohort of filles du roi have arrived. Talon expresses his dissatisfaction with the large quantity of women Colbert has sent over. Talon would prefer for the women to have received a sufficient education to make it easier for him to make appropriate matches between the ladies and the male colonists in terms of social structure. The letter highlights the way different women are treated in the society based on opportunity. Textbooks often highlight the founding fathers of newly born nations, but women are often not published into the equation. Talon's letter exemplifies this by showing the ways in which women were viewed as passive administrative commodities instead of pioneers or key figures in society.
