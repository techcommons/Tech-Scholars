Filles Du Roi, Rings and Rivers

France's 17th century initiative to populate Quebec.

In the early 1600s, France and England were long at competition with each other as both countries sought to expand their colonies to exploit the beautiful, and, to them, newly discovered land. To grow the population of New France, King Louis XIV sponsored young women to immigrate across the Atlantic to North America. In return the families of these young ladies would receive a dowry. The dowry was huge encouragement to fathers living in France who had fallen on hard times as a result of crop failures and continuous warfare. The daughters of these families did not have much choice in terms of moving to the new world, but they were allowed to refuse proposals to men they did not want to marry in New France. These women, known as Filles du roi or The King's Daughters, would marry French colonists and give birth to a new generation to help populate the struggling colony.
