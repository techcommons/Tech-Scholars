Our Classroom

My Project

My name is Justin Wang, a teen creator who built this website as part of the Tech Scholars: Web Design program at the New-York Historical Society. During this one-week intensive, our cohort learned how to use HTML and CSS to design, code, and build our own websites. Our goal was to explore how women across the spectrum of race, class, and sexuality exercised power and effected change. Alongside our coding sessions, we toured museum galleries with curators and met with a professional from the Bard Graduate Center, who showed us what an exciting future in digital interventions and the digital humanities can look like.

Why Honeywell?

Working within the Tech Commons, an incubator for youth-driven digital history projects, I chose to focus my research on Martha Ann Honeywell. Martha was a remarkable self-employed performer in early America who made a lasting impact despite navigating significant disabilities. Her journey perfectly encapsulates the spirit of a digital changemaker, and this project aims to amplify her powerful story by combining modern digital tools with historical research.

As you explore this site, I invite you to consider a few questions: How did women like Martha Ann Honeywell redefine success and independence in early America? In what ways do her experiences with disability and self-employment challenge our traditional understanding of historical narratives? I hope her legacy inspires you as much as it has inspired me.
