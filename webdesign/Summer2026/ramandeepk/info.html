Hidden Figures that Drove the Household and Economy
Who?

When recounting the American Revolution, history often highlights the Sons of Liberty as one of the most courageous revolutionary groups, specifically for their role in the Boston Tea Party. Dumping tea into Boston Harbor was one of the many acts of defiance that American colonists carried out against Britain to protest rising taxes and the Tea Act of 1773. However, the contributions of American women are overlooked. Female colonists formed their own coalition, the Daughters of Liberty, to not only support the Sons of Liberty but to also express their own opposition to British rule.

What?

During the revolutionary era, women had little financial or legal power. Most women could not own property or control major financial decisions. Nevertheless, the domestic role of women to manage their households gave them economic power in their daily lives. Women decided what products were being purchased, what businesses were supported and brought into their home. During the Revolution, patriot women utilized this power to boycott British goods and to buy specific products that reflected their pro-American ideals. Goods such as British textiles and tea were boycotted throughout entire communities, while special teapots with patriotic slogans reflected support for the revolutionary cause. This reinforced revolutionary ideals within the household and demonstrated the family's support for independence.

Why?

Ultimately, women played an important role during the Boston Tea Party and the fight for independence. Although their contributions are often overlooked, their support and influence helped shape the events of the American Revolution. With this newfound information, I am left wondering how women had gained specific financial power in a time of strict coverture and limitations in society. By learning about their stories, we can bring their important role in history to light.
