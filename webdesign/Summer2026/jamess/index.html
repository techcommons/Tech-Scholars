Johanna De Laet

Johanna De Laet was born in Leiden, the Netherlands, on September 6th 1623, her father was a man named Johannes De Laet, a popular geographer and a director/ founder of the Dutch west india company who wrote the book History of the new world, one of the most comprehensive and complete books on the Americas at the time. And her mother was Maria Boudewijns, a housewife from Germany. As a child Johanna received an above average education, which was remarkable for a woman of the time, but the dutch were known for being more liberal with women's rights at the time. At the young age of 21 on the 16th of June 1644 she married Johan De Hulter, a director and colleague of Johanne de Laet (her father) at the Dutch West India Company (Yes, I know its a lot of Johan-ish names, believe me, it only gets worse) Together, they had a daughter named Johannas De Hulter, around the year 1645. So for the next 5-6 years she enjoyed the luxury of being a wealthy dutch housewife in her hometown of Leiden. In her home and traditional Dutch culture she was in charge of raising and taking care of the children and other household duties. But in 1649 her life was changed for better or worse when her father, who was a very rich man died, leaving her with a handsome inheritance, including a parcel of land in what is now Kingston, NY but was then known as Rensselaerswyck, New Netherlands, funnily enough, Johanne himself never did visit the Americas. Johanna and her family sailed to the Americas on a ship called the Graft. When they got to the new world, they settled native land that they purchased from the Esopus people, a group of natives, in what is now Hudson Valley. With them to the new world, the couple brought bricklayers and other craftsmen with them to settle New Amsterdam, Kingston, and the Esopus, as it is called. In this time, they quickly made themselves at home,getting involved in politics, opening businesses, and constructing buildings, as Johan himself was a bricklayer, and a member of the town council in New Amsterdam, mainly because he was a director at the Dutch West India Company, so it can be assumed that he was only appointed for his wealth. Johanna herself also took responsibility, taking care of the child, their massive house (believe me), and minor affairs such as their never ending feud with their neighbors (allegedly) and the baptization of her daughter in 1650. Unfortunately though, this time of wealth and prosperity came to an end in 1655 when her beloved husband, Johan died from disease. She inherited 500 Morgens of land (300 ish acres) from him at the native land that they settled, her child, Johannes De Hulter (gee how original), also inherited land, but she was 9 years old at the time so she managed her inheritance and held on to it for her. But this change gave her an opportunity to showcase her business skills as she was now the sole caretaker of her child, she needed to feed and support her family so she took ownership of some of her husband's businesses, such as a textile company and bakery. She also rented out homes to people and traded in beaver pelts in addition to selling most of her husband's assets. In 1658 she remarried to Jeronimus Ebbing, an economist and politician in New Amsterdam,They had 3 kids and moved to Fort Orange. another nearby settlement just south, where she continued to manage her business but now under the surname of Ebbing. In fort orange where she and her shiny new husband lived the rest of their lives but she maintained ties with the Esopus, kingston, and new amsterdam as her appearances were spread across these places.Even though she remarried, she did not forsake her business ventures and did not let her having a husband make her lose her individuality and ability to do what she wanted. She continued to partake in legal dispuetes until 1676, at the age of 53, in which she sold her fathers inherited land in New Netherland, but she was never directly mentioned in any political court recors again, besides death certificates, birth certificates, weddings and the like. She died 7 years later in 1660
