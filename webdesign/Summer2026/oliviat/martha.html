Martha Wright Griffiths

Martha Wright Griffiths, born January 19, 1912 in Pierce City, Missouri, was a pivotal figure in the passage of women's rights legislation throughout the mid-to-late twentieth century. Griffiths' introduction to politics began with an unsuccessful bid for a seat in the Michigan House of Representatives in 1946. However, she would later go on to win an election for the state legislature from 1948–1950, and capture a Democratic nomination for a seat in the U.S. Congress in 1952 ("Griffiths"). Griffiths is credited with making a wildly effective political argument in favor of adding the word "sex" to the Civil Rights Act of 1964. She is also regarded as a significant advocate and sponsor of the Equal Rights Amendment, successfully guiding its passage through the House of Representatives in 1973 ("Martha"). Griffiths first re-introduced the Equal Rights Amendment to the House in 1970 under a rare discharge petition ("Representative"). She faced a multitude of backlash and opposition from Republican congressmembers; however, Griffiths' advocacy for the ERA and women's rights displays a strong sense of courage and commitment.
