Equal Rights for All

The Equal Rights Amendment (ERA) has been one of the longest-running constitutional movements in American history. For over a century, courageous women and allies fought to guarantee that equality under the law could not be denied because of sex.

Meet the Pioneers

Project Overview

As of July 2026, the U.S. Constitution does not explicitly guarantee equal rights on the basis of sex. Considering the prolonged championing of the Equal Rights Amendment through the efforts of early progressive suffragists and trailblazers of the Second-Wave Feminist Era, is the ratification of the ERA conceivable in today's current political climate?

This website explores the influential women whose leadership, activism, and determination inspired generations of Americans to continue fighting for equal rights.

ERA Timeline

1848

The Seneca Falls Convention launches the organized women's rights movement.

1923

Alice Paul introduces the first Equal Rights Amendment.

1972

Congress passes the Equal Rights Amendment and sends it to the states.

2020

Virginia becomes the 38th state to ratify the ERA.
