Crystal Eastman

Crystal Eastman, born June 25, 1881 in Marlborough, Massachusetts, was one of the most outspoken Progressive reformers of the early twentieth century. Eastman was a feminist who assumed a vital role in the women's suffrage movement, and is best-known as being a co-founder of the National Woman's Party with Alice Paul. ("Women's"). Analogous to Paul, Crystal Eastman sought to achieve complete, nation-wide gender equality even after the ratification of the nineteenth amendment in 1920. In her timely address, "Now We Can Begin," Eastman set forth her blueprint, rooted in the principles of feminism, to achieve widespread equality. She called for equal pay, and an end to gender-related discrimination in the workplace. In order to further these causes, Eastman co-authored the first Equal Rights Amendment along with Alice Paul, which would later be introduced to the Senate in 1923 ("Crystal").
