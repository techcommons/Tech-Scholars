Alice Paul

Alice Stokes Paul, born January 11, 1885, was first introduced to the women's rights movement through her mother, Tracie Perry Paul, an outspoken suffragist and member of the National American Woman Suffrage Association (NAWSA). Following the ratification of the nineteenth amendment in 1920, which granted women the right to vote, Paul drafted an Equal Rights Amendment that aimed to outlaw discrimination based on sex ("Handbill"). In 1923, she introduced the first ever Equal Rights Amendment to ensure permanent equality of the sexes as a constitutional protection.
