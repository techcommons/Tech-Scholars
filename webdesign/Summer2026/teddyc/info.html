Important Figures

Clarah Lemlich Shavelson

Institution: WAMS

Clara Lemlich was born in 1886 in Gorodok, Ukraine. After her family immigrated to the U.S. in order to escape antisemitism, Shavelson joined the garment industry. Shavelson's father struggled to find work, meaning she was the primary breadwinner of the family. Despite her tireless work in the garment industry, Shavelson was also an incessant reader and found time to attend night school after her grueling shifts, both of which helped her form distinct opinions about the rights of the working class. Shavelson immediately recognized the need for garment workers to unionize. With much determination, taking advantage of her superb public speaking skills and her shared experiences with other workers, Shavelson helped found the International Ladies' Garment Workers' Union. Shavelson was fired multiple times, eventually being banned from the garment industry altogether. During one strike, Shavelson was arrested seventeen times and the police broke six of her ribs. Within days, she was back on the picket line.

Rose Pesotta

Rose Pesotta addresses the floor at the 1965 ILGWU convention, December 15, 1965. Institution: Kheel Center, Cornell University

Rose Pesotta, born Rachelle Peisoty on November 20, 1896 (her name was recorded incorrectly at Ellis Island), was the second oldest of ten children. She emigrated to the U.S. at sixteen to escape an arranged marriage. In 1919, her father was murdered by Ukrainian nationalists. Rose's sister found her a job in a shirtwaist factory organized by the ILGWU. She quickly became an organizer for the union, participating in many strikes and establishing one of the first worker education programs. Pesotta was the first female president of the ILGWU. Later in her career, she assisted other unions in their collective bargaining efforts such as the rubber workers during their sit-down strike at Goodyear in Akron, Ohio and the auto workers during their sit-down at General Motors in Flint, Michigan. Finally, she became an activist and an author, primarily speaking out against antisemitism and racism.
