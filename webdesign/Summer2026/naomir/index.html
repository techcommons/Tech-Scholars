About Amelia Simmons

In 1776, a woman named Amelia Simmons, otherwise known as "an American Orphan," gave a new nation its first cookbook written by and published by an American. Simmons identified herself on the title page of American Cookery as "an American orphan." Beyond that declaration, the historical record on Simmons is remarkably quiet. She has no authenticated portrait, birth date, or death date.

Scholars infer from her writing that Simmons worked as a domestic cook, likely in New England, but her exact circumstances remain unknown. Her book, American Cookery, addressed "young females" responsible for household work and adapted inherited English culinary practices to the ingredients, tools, and conditions of the new republic.

American Cookery marked a turning point in American history because households began to rely on an American written guide instead of British cookbooks. Her work helped establish an American style of cooking and became an important part of the nation's early history.
