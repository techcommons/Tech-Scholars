Judy Chicago

During her career, Chicago pioneered Feminist art and art education. Born in Chicago in 1939, she later attended the Art Institute of Chicago and the University of California, Los Angeles. Believing in the need for a feminist pedagogy for female art students, she began the first Feminist Art Program at California State University in 1970. The following year, with artist Miriam Schapiro, she co-founded the Feminist Art Program at California Institute of the Arts, Valencia.

The Dinner Party




The Dinner Party (1974 to 1979) is arguably Chicago's most well-known piece. This was a collaborative project that incorporates traditional women's crafts such as embroidery, needlepoint, and ceramics. The Dinner Party is a massive ceremonial banquet, arranged on a triangular table with a total of thirty-nine place settings, each commemorating an important woman from history. The settings consist of embroidered runners, gold chalices and utensils, and china-painted porcelain plates with raised central motifs that are based on vulvar and butterfly forms and rendered in styles appropriate to the individual women being honored.
