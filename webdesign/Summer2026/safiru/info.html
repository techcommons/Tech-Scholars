Information

Shared Tombstone of Charity and Sylvia, June 20,2025, Vermont Public, Weybridge, Vermont.

Charity Bryant (1777-1851) and Sylvia Drake (1784-1868) were a same-sex couple who lived together in Weybridge Massachusetts long before same-sex marriage's were allowed in the United States. They met in 1807 after Charity moved to Vermont. By July of 1807, they shared a household and ran a joint tailoring business. Their neighbors and community accepted them as a married couple so much so they share a single headstone.

Their lives are important for American women's history because they demonstrate how women built economic independence and a romantic relationship with another woman long before modern LGBTQ+ rights movements.Charity and Sylvia's household show a rare look into women's work and same-sex relationships in early America.

One significant part of Charity Bryant and Sylvia Drake's life was the tailoring business they built together . By working as partners, they earned their own income and achieved a level of financial independence that was unusual for women in the early 1800s. Their successful business allowed them to support themselves without relying on a husband and also strengthened their commitment to one another. The shop became a symbol their shared life and showed that two women could build a stable home, contribute to their community, and be respected for both their skills and their partnership in the early 19th century.
